Trial planner

·Bearing unpacking, at-speed completion

Real-robot trials are the scarce resource. This page answers the question each study actually asks, forecasts a new checkpoint from its pre-training quality before it touches the robot, and plans where the next trials should go.

What this finds on the published counts:

    Is the trend real?

    Pairwise letters ask whether two specific checkpoints differ. A scaling claim asks whether performance rises along an ordered axis. With the same trials, a trend test answers that question directly.

    Forecast a checkpoint before it touches the robot

    Pre-training quality (DINO FD on held-out web video) ranked all seven checkpoints in the order the robot did. Fitting that relationship turns a new checkpoint's FD into a forecast with honest uncertainty, and into the number of trials needed to show it beats the incumbent.

    Spend the next trials

    Where trials go matters as much as how many. For a trend question, trials at the ends of the sweep carry the most information; a middle point keeps the ability to see saturation.

    Looking early

    Stopping a clearly worse policy early saves trials, but fixed-n tests assume the trial count was set in advance. If the letters are recomputed after every session, the 5% family-wise error no longer holds.
